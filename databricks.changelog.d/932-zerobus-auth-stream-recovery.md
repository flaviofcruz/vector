Let the `databricks_zerobus` sink recover from an auth/token-poisoned stream instead of rejecting every batch until pod restart. The SDK retains a multiplexed stream's terminal lane failure and replays it for every later ingest, and classifies token-mint 4xx (`InvalidUCTokenError`) and gRPC `UNAUTHENTICATED`/`PERMISSION_DENIED` non-retryable, so a transient token-mint 401 discarded all subsequent batches (prod aws ap-southeast-2, 2026-11-10: ~92M events lost as `invalid_config` while mints had already recovered). These errors are now retryable at the sink level: batches stay unacked and are held by the LP deployment's unbounded request retries, blocking (`when_full=block`) buffers, and blob-queue redelivery until the stream recovers. The failing stream is discarded, and the rebuild is paced by a shared backoff (1s doubled per consecutive failure, capped at 60s, jittered; reset on success). `PERMISSION_DENIED` is included deliberately — grant/RAR propagation can be transient — with a known trade-off: a permanently revoked grant now retries indefinitely and can stall the pod, so alert on `zerobus_auth_stream_discard_total` and `zerobus_auth_stream_build_failure_total`. `TokenFetchError` (mint 5xx/network) already discards a closed mux via the pre-existing retryable-and-closed rule, unchanged, as are table/schema and encoding errors. New counters `zerobus_auth_stream_discard_total{error_code}`, `zerobus_auth_stream_build_failure_total{error_code}` (a failed stream build, the only signal when the very first build fails and no stream ever exists to discard), and `zerobus_auth_stream_rebuild_total` make the failure and the recovery observable.
